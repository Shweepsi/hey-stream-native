Privacy policy

Effective October 30, 2026

In short: Hey Stream has no account, no ads and no analytics. Your credentials stay on your device. Only anonymous crash reports may be sent, and you can turn them off. The app also asks GitHub whether a new version exists.

What the app keeps on your device

  • Your subscription’s server address, username and password, encrypted with an Android system key that cannot be exported.
  • Your subscription’s catalog, your favorites, recent channels and titles, resume positions, titles marked as watched, hidden categories and your parental code.
  • Only if you turn on the test tools (hidden, off by default): a copy of your provider’s responses, from which the app removes the username, password, server address and IP addresses. It only leaves the device if you share it yourself.

This data does not leave the device: it is excluded from Android backups and from device-to-device transfer. Signing out erases it, and so does uninstalling the app.

Who the app talks to

Your IPTV provider

The app only connects to the server you entered, to check your subscription, download the catalog (once a day and when you ask), and load programs, images and videos. This traffic goes straight from your device to the provider, without going through any Hey Stream server (there is none). The app identifies itself as “HeyStream” followed by its version. Your provider handles this data under its own policy.

GitHub, for updates

At most once a day, and when you ask in Settings › About, the app asks GitHub for the list of the project’s published versions. If you choose to install a new version, it downloads it from GitHub. These requests contain no data about you or your subscription; like any online service, GitHub receives the connection’s IP address (see GitHub’s privacy statement).

Tester version

The above applies to the public version. The tester version (file -testeur.apk) is only given to testers who have agreed to it. It sends usage data to the project’s collection service, hosted by Cloudflare: provider responses with username, password, server address and IP addresses removed by the app; screens opened, searches, playback (start-up, interruptions, formats, duration), errors, crashes, device model and memory. Never a stream address or a username; the device is identified by a random number. It also checks for updates with this service rather than GitHub. This data is kept for one year. To stop, install the public version or ask the person running the test to turn off collection for your device.

GlitchTip, for crash reports

When the app crashes, freezes, hits an internal error or has to give up on playback after several retries, it may send a report to GlitchTip, an error tracking service, so the problem can be fixed.

A report contains: what the error was and where in the code it happened, the app and Android versions, the device model, its language and technical state (for example free memory, battery level, orientation), and for abandoned playback, only the player’s error code.

A report does not contain: your credentials, your server address, stream links, what you watched, or any account or installation identifier. Before sending, the app strips web addresses, server names and IP addresses from every message. Like any online service, GlitchTip receives the IP address of the connection that sends the report; the app does not attach it to the report.

Reports are only sent by published builds and are on by default. You can turn them off at any time in Settings › About › Send anonymous crash reports: nothing more is sent from that moment. They are only used to fix the app (legitimate interest: its stability) and are kept by GlitchTip for a limited time.

This website

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies, no scripts and no resources from other sites. GitHub may log visitors’ IP addresses for the security of its service (see the GitHub privacy statement).

Your rights

You have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, objection and restriction under the GDPR. The app’s data stays on your device, where you erase it by signing out or uninstalling the app. Since crash reports are anonymous, we cannot link them to you. For any question, open an issue on GitHub. You may also lodge a complaint with your data protection authority (in France, the CNIL).

Changes

Any change to this policy is published on this page with its new date; the full history is in the project repository.
